# Research: how to nail "know the letters" (5 Nov 2026)

## Key findings
- **Ministry of Education definition** of letter knowledge in kindergarten: the letter's **shape, name, and the link between shape and sound**, plus alphabet order and early "phonetic writing" of familiar words. Phonological awareness goes from syllables to smaller sounds. ([MoE kindergarten](https://pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/magar-ganey-yeladim/tashtit-kria), [MoE reading/writing acquisition](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/acquisition-reading-writing.pdf), [MoE grade 1](https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/ivrit_chinhch_leshony/yesodi/pedagogy-hebrew-linguistic-education/teaching-hebrew-grade1))
- **Israeli kindergartners know names much better than sounds.** Hebrew letter names start with the letter's sound, and children reach the sound through the name, often answering with consonant + vowel ("בֵּ" for ב). Hardest: final letters and letters that look like many others. ([Treiman, Levin & Kessler 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278080))
- **Writing in kindergarten predicts grade-1 reading and spelling**, best with an adult's guidance. ([Levin, Aram et al.](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/on-the-contribution-of-kindergarten-writing-to-grade-1-literacy-a/))
- **Free writing beats tracing** for learning to recognize letters. ([James & Engelhardt](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4274624))
- **Pictures drawn inside the letter's shape** (e.g. a letter that is also an object starting with its sound) beat separate pictures for Hebrew letter–sound learning. ([Shmidman & Ehri 2010](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/embedded-picture-mnemonics-to-learn-letters/811797128148942848-9814))
- **Hebrew phonological awareness is different from English:** consonant + vowel chunks (בָּ in בַּיִת) and final sounds are easier than rhymes, so rhyme games are a weak fit. ([Kogan & Saiegh-Haddad](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/45B440E8E540DDE11005B975D1D3DBE9/S0142716423000012a.pdf/internal_structure_of_the_syllable_in_russian_and_in_hebrew_evidence_from_monolingual_kindergarteners.pdf))
- **Several fast passes through the alphabet beat one slow "letter of the week".** ([Jones & Reutzel 2012](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/teal_facpub/449), [Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/resources/resource-library/enhancing-alphabet-knowledge-instruction-research-implications-and))
- **Common criticism of reading apps:** too much game around too little teaching, only letter names, kids "tap blindly" to move on. ([Mentava](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.mentava.com/blog/false-promises-the-reality-behind-educational-reading-apps))

## Prioritized improvements (✅ = done)
1. **Teach the letter's sound, not only its name** ("בֵּית… בּ… בַּיִת"). This is the biggest gap. *Mom decides: a bare consonant, or consonant + vowel?*
2. **New question type: hear the sound, pick the letter.**
3. **Picture mnemonics drawn into the letter.** *Mom's stories plus an illustrator.*
4. **Real mastery:** right answers on different days and in at least 2 question types, shown to parents as green/yellow/gray.
5. **Review in cycles:** all letters met so far, weighted toward weak ones, with gaps of about 1, 3 and 7 days.
6. **Lookalike duels** (ד/ר, ה/ח, ב/כ, ו/ז) focused on the telling difference.
   - ✅ Sound-alike letters (ט/ת, כּ/ק, א/ע) are never wrong answers in "what starts with…" (fixed 5 Nov).
7. **Final letters as the end-of-word forms** of letters the child knows, plus a "start or end of the word?" game.
8. **Last-sound and syllable games** (clap the syllables, "what does it end with?"); these also bridge to kamatz/patach.
9. **Free writing with a gentle check** (being built).
10. **Write my name** (a Ministry kindergarten goal; highly motivating).
11. **The dot in בּ/כּ/פּ:** teach the dotted sound first, "without the dot it says v" later. *Mom decides.*
12. **Teaching order:** distinct and frequent letters early, lookalikes spread apart. *Mom decides.*
13. **Silent letters א/ע** ("they borrow the vowel's sound"). *Mom's wording.*
14. **Say it aloud, then tap to hear if you were right** (a self-check, no speech recognition).
15. **One off-screen idea per letter for parents** ("find 3 things in the kitchen that start with מ").

## Game ideas specific to Hebrew letters
1. **The finals' pajamas:** at the end of a word, the letter puts on its "pajamas" (the final form) before going to sleep.
2. **Spot the difference** with a magnifying glass on lookalike letters.
3. **Dot or no dot** (בּ/ב), with a "b" monster and a "v" monster. *After mom approves.*
4. **The quiet letters** א/ע borrow a voice from a vowel sticker (a bridge to nikud).
5. **Letter hunt in a picture scene** ("find things that start with שׁ").
