## Part 1. Talking to mom: interview guide

### The one rule: listen first, show the app last
- The first 30–40 minutes are about **her classroom, not our app**. Don't show the iPad yet. If we show it first,
  she'll react to our screens ("nice!") instead of telling us how she really teaches.
  *(customer-interviews: "avoid the sales pitch"; first "interviews" that show the product give polite noise, not signal.)*
- Ask about **real, recent moments**, not opinions. "Tell me about the last time…" beats "How do you usually…".
  Then follow the story step by step: "and then what happened?" *(Teresa Torres, story-based interviewing.)*
- Ask "why?" and "show me" a lot. Ask her to **say** things the way she says them in class (the letter sound, her
  praise, her hand movement). Record the meeting on your phone (ask her first): her exact words are content.
- Look for **what surprises us** and what proves our drafts wrong. *(Shaun Clowes: actively seek disproof.)*
  Every "no, I'd never do it like that" is worth more than ten "that's nice".
- Stay quiet after a question. Count to 5 in your head. The best answers come after the pause.

Bring: a notebook, the phone to record, a pen and paper for her to draw letters, the iPad (charged, sound on,
on the live site with a fresh test child), the list of decisions at the end of this section.

### 1. A real lesson, step by step (15 min)
1. "Take me back to the first week of grade 1 last year (or the last class you taught). The very first reading lesson.
   What did the kids see when they walked in? What did you say first?" Then: "and then?" until the lesson ends.
2. "The first letter you taught: which one, and why that one?" "Show me how you introduced it. Say it like you said it to them."
3. "What did the kids **do** with their hands and voices in that lesson? (said it, wrote it, moved, sang, drew…)"
4. "How long was it? When did you see them get tired? What did you do then?"
5. "What did you send home that day?"
6. "Tell me about the first time in the year a child **read a word alone**. Which word? What happened in the class?"

### 2. The letter sound (the biggest open question) (5 min)
7. "When you teach ב, what exactly do you say? Say it to me." (Listen: "בֵּית"? "בּ" alone? "בָּ"? Write down exactly.)
8. "When does a child first hear the letter's **sound** and not only its name? How do you explain the difference?"
9. "And א / ע, the quiet letters: what do you say about them?" "בּ/ב, כּ/כ, פּ/פ: when do you teach the dot?"
10. "Do you have a story, a picture or a hand movement for each letter? For each vowel? Show me two."

### 3. Where kids get stuck, and what she does (10 min)
11. "Think of a child last year who had a hard time with reading. Tell me about him or her. When did you first notice?"
12. "What did you do the next day? What worked? What didn't?"
13. "Which letters do kids mix up the most? Which vowels? What do you do in the moment a child says the wrong one?"
14. "What does a child do when he's stuck and doesn't want you to see it?" (guessing, copying, looking at the picture…)
    This tells us how kids will "game" our app too.
15. "The jump from syllables (בָּ) to a whole word (בַּיִת): where do kids fall? How do you carry them over?"

### 4. How she knows a child "knows" (5 min)
16. "How do you know a child really knows a letter, not just guessed? What do you check?"
17. "How do you know a child knows a vowel? Reads a word? Reads a sentence?"
18. "Has a child ever 'known' something one week and forgotten it the next? What did you do?"
19. "Does writing a letter count as knowing it, for you?"
    (Our draft rule: right on the first try, on 2 different days, in 2 kinds of question. Don't say it yet; ask later.)

### 5. Her order (5 min)
20. "If you had to write your year on one page, what's the order? Letters in what order? Vowels in what order?"
    Let her write it on paper. Ask her to bring her old lesson plans or the book she uses, if she has them.
21. "Do you teach letters all first, and then vowels? Or letters and vowels together?"
    (A graded series teaches שְׁוָא with קָמָץ/פַּתָּח. Ask what she does, and why.)
22. "Which letters do you keep apart on purpose? Which do you teach early because they're easy?"
23. "What must a child know on the **first day of grade 1** so you'd say 'he already reads'?"

### 6. Parents (5 min)
24. "What do parents ask you at the first parents' meeting? What are they worried about?"
25. "Tell me about a parent who tried to teach their child at home and it went wrong. What happened?"
26. "What do you wish every parent did at home, 10 minutes a day?"
27. "Which apps or YouTube videos do parents tell you about? What do you think of them? Why?"
28. "What would a parent need to see to trust that a child is learning, not just playing?"

### 7. What she'd never want (3 min)
29. "Imagine an app that teaches reading. What would make you angry if you saw it on a child's iPad?"
30. "Is there anything we must **never** say to a child who got it wrong?"
31. "How long should a 5-year-old sit with this a day? When should it tell them to stop?"

### 8. What makes her proud (3 min)
32. "Tell me about a moment in your career you're really proud of." (Listen for what she values: this is our brand.)
33. "What do former students or parents thank you for, years later?"
34. "If a child finishes this app and meets you in grade 1, what would make you say 'wow'?"

### 9. Show her the app (20 min), stay quiet while she uses it
**Before:** a fresh test child on the live site, sound on, practice mode on. Say only:
"I'll give it to you as if you were a 5-year-old. Think out loud. I won't help, that's on purpose."
Then **stop talking**. Don't explain, don't defend, don't point. If she asks "what do I do?", reply "what would you try?"
*(customer-interviews: direct observation beats reports; watch micro-reactions.)*

Show, in this order (one tap path each, about 3 min each):
1. **First visit** → setup → pick the buddy → the big map. Watch: does she understand where to go, without you?
2. **Letter station ב** (station 1): 📖 learn → 🎯 quiz → ✍️ write. Watch her face at the letter name, the example
   word, the sound. Watch how she holds the "pen" in writing and whether she agrees with the stroke order.
3. **A game** (🔍 lookalike detective) and the 🎁 prize. Watch: does she see learning or just a game?
4. **Nikud: kamatz/patach world**, the syllable builder and "read alone". This is closest to her real job.
5. **One story** (kamatz/patach has real audio). Does the Hebrew sound right to her? Would she use it in class?
6. **Parents' area** (🔒, code 17). Would she trust this as a parent? What would she want to see?

Watch for and write down (time + screen):
- Where she **pauses**, frowns, laughs, says "no" or "hmm", or starts to correct the screen ("we say it like…").
- Where she **takes over as a teacher** (says the letter louder, points, shows a hand movement). Those are her method
  showing; each one is a feature.
- Any word, picture or sound she'd change. Write it next to the screen name.
After she's done, only then ask: "What's the first thing you'd change?" "What's missing that you do in class?"
"Would you give this to a niece? Why / why not?" (Real pull = she wants to show it to someone, or asks when it's ready.)

### 10. The decisions we need from her (checklist)
Tick each one in the meeting or set a date. Short answers are fine; "I'll think about it" goes on a list.

**Her method**
- [ ] The **letter sound**: bare "בּ", "בָּ", or through the name? How she says it out loud (record her saying 3 letters).
- [ ] **Letter order** (write it down). Which lookalikes are kept apart.
- [ ] **Vowel order**, and whether שְׁוָא comes with קָמָץ/פַּתָּח (as in the published series) or last (as now).
- [ ] Letters first then vowels, or together? Does the 3-chapter map (letters → nikud with words and sentences inside → "I read" books) match her year?
- [ ] א/ע (quiet letters) wording, and when to teach the dot in בּ/כּ/פּ.
- [ ] A **story, picture or hand movement** per letter and per vowel (these become her video clips).
- [ ] Is "which letter does אַרְיֵה start with?" fair for silent א/ע?

**Content to review (drafts)**
- [ ] Example word per letter (הַר, וָפֶל, זַיִת, טְרַקְטוֹר, לַוְיָתָן, מַטְרִיָּה, סַנְדָּל, פַּרְפַּר, קַקְטוּס, שָׁעוֹן…). Are they words a 5-year-old knows, and fair for the letter's sound?
- [ ] **Writing strokes** for all 27 letters (`/studio` → "איך כותבים כל אות"). Order, direction, start point.
- [ ] How **strict** free writing should be; should writing count toward "knows"?
- [ ] Nikud worlds: letters per station, words, how she describes each mark (kamatz = "a line with a dot under it"?).
- [ ] The 36 stories: is the Hebrew natural? Are they decodable the way she'd want?
- [ ] The finals' "pajamas" story, the hunt scenes' words, the wording "אֵיזוֹ אוֹת? תַּגִּיד בְּקוֹל!".

**The mastery rule**
- [ ] "Knows" = right first try, 2 different days, 2 kinds of question. Is that her bar? Should a later miss take it back?
- [ ] Review after about 1, 3, 7 days: does that match what she sees in class?

**Mom in the app (docs/08)**
- [ ] What the kids call her ("הַמּוֹרָה ___"?).
- [ ] **Consent in writing**: recorded, filmed, voice-cloned; product only or marketing too; what if she wants out later.
- [ ] She records the "must be perfect" set: 27 letter names, about 250 syllables, about 200 words (1–1.5 h, can be split).
- [ ] Her favorite praise lines (15 min) and 10–15 short phone clips (welcome, each vowel, end of a world).
- [ ] A smiling photo for the guide bubble (replaces 👩‍🏫).
- [ ] How many hours a week she can give, and the first date for a recording session.

**Pilot**
- [ ] Can she name **5 families** with a child in גן חובה who'd try it for 3 weeks? (See the pilot plan in Part 2.)
- [ ] Would she do a 10-minute "reading check" with each pilot child before and after (she knows how to test a child)?

### After the meeting (same evening, 20 min)
Write down, while it's fresh: (1) the 3 things that surprised you, (2) her exact words for the letter sound, praise
and each vowel, (3) every "no" she said about the app, (4) her answers to the checklist. Send it to Claude and
we update `content/` and `docs/STATUS.md` from it.
